# The Metabarons — Complete Lore Reference

*Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky. Illustrated by Juan Giménez (main saga), Travis Charest, Zoran Janjetov (Weapons), and Das Pastoras (Castaka). Published by Les Humanoïdes Associés, 1992–2008. English editions by Humanoids Publishing.*

---

## Overview

The Saga of the Metabarons is an eight-volume epic tracing five generations of a warrior dynasty from its accidental founding to its deliberate extinction. It is narrated retrospectively by Tonto, a robot servant waiting in the Metabunker for a master who may never return, to his companion Lothar — who is, unknowingly, the dynasty's third generation in a wiped and re-housed body.

The dynasty is not simply a line of warriors. It is a philosophical machine: each generation must prove legitimacy through a witnessed, irreversible bodily cost, must kill the previous titleholder to inherit the title, and must produce an heir capable of killing them. The law governing this is the Bushitaka. Every generation tries to escape it. None succeed until the last.

---

## The Framing Device — Tonto and Lothar

The entire saga is narrated from inside the Metabunker, the mobile fortress that serves as the dynasty's home and the Metabaron's base of operations, anchored in City Shaft — a vertical urban habitat.

**Tonto** is a domestic robot who has served the dynasty for generations. He carries the dynasty's oral history in his circuits and narrates it to Lothar to pass time during their master's three-month absence, which stretches across centuries of story-time. Tonto's knowledge is partly firsthand observation, partly inherited record. He suppresses certain truths for dramatic and protective reasons — he knows the secret of Lothar's identity long before he reveals it.

**Lothar** is presented throughout as a simple companion robot, curious and sometimes foolish. He interrupts the story, asks naive questions, and provides Tonto's excuse to slow down and explain. His clumsiness accidentally triggers crises — activating the Metabaron's bio-electrogram, which damages the Metabunker, nearly kills them, and once slices the fortress in half.

The revelation of the final volume is that Lothar is not a robot at all. He is Steelhead — the third-generation Metabaron — whose indestructible body was encased in heavy metallic armor and whose memory was surgically erased twice by the last Metabaron to prevent him from being used as a weapon. He has been listening to his own family's story without knowing it. He gave the last Metabaron the scar above his right eyebrow. He sacrificed himself at the end to give his grandson something clean to stand on.

## Part One — The Castaka Origin

### The Castaka Clan

The dynasty begins not with the Metabarons themselves but with the noble **Castaka clan**, whose ancient history is told in *The Crest of Castaka* (the short story included in the special volume *The House of the Ancestors*) and *Castaka* (the two-volume prequel series illustrated by Das Pastoras).

The Castakas are a noble warrior lineage who live on **Ahour**, a small fertile planet. Their distinguishing mark — the Crest of Castaka — is a living seal burned into the chest of each hereditary ruler, passed from parent to child at the moment of succession. It does not merely symbolize lineage; it carries the accumulated memories, knowledge, and souls of all prior ancestors. The holder of the crest can draw on that inherited wisdom. It is the dynasty's most sacred inheritance and the one thing that cannot be taken by force.

### The Origin of the Crest — Dayal and Gangez

The crest's origin predates recorded Castaka history. On the marble planet **Marmola**, Dayal of Castaka's family encountered Gangez, the Millennial Angel Vulture — a megabird of near-divine stature nearing death. Three thieves called the Three Aces (Kouhry, Lahry, Mohry) tried to use the corpse of Dayal's wife Antigrea as bait to capture Gangez and force him to reveal a buried treasure. Dayal killed the thieves and fed them to the dying bird instead. Moved by Dayal's generosity, Gangez — who had swallowed Antigrea — used his last strength to guide them to Marmola's only valley of fertile earth, the one place where burial per the Bushitaka code was possible. Dayal buried Gangez there. Impressed by the Castaka's adherence to the burial rite, Gangez offered his soul to Dayal to live within him as master and guardian of the planet's treasure. Dayal accepted. Gangez transferred his crest into Dayal's chest. The soul of Gangez then guided the Castakas to the heart of Marmola, where they discovered **epyphite**: the sacred anti-gravity oil that would define and endanger their clan for generations.

### Dayal of Castaka — The First Ancestor

Baron **Dayal of Castaka** is the true founding ancestor, born of violence: the Amakura clan waged war against the Castakas on Ahour, and the Castaka prince Divadal — losing the war — took prisoner Oriela, wife of the Amakura king Omezo. Divadal impregnated Oriela and asked her to kill him once the bloodline was secured. By Bushitaka law, King Omezo should have demanded Oriela's immediate death after liberation, but waited for the birth. Oriela named the boy Dayal, left the scar of her teeth on him as a memento, and ritually committed suicide when Dayal was eight years old.

Dayal grew within the Castaka palace, forced to ritually inseminate three Castaka women every night for twelve years to rebuild the depleted clan. The loveless mechanical reproduction made him a cruel lover. At twenty-six he received the title of Baron. When General Pakko revealed the truth of his parentage, Dayal went mad. Only the arrival of Antigrea — a duchess his age, trained in combat — pulled him back. They fell in love. Antigrea bore him twin daughters, Myrtha and Narda, before becoming barren.

The **Techno-techno sect** then built an artificial planet in the same orbit as Ahour to house their Techno-pope, decided the existing population of Ahour was a threat, and disintegrated the entire planet. Dayal's family survived only because their hunter's den was embedded in a rock fragment that drifted intact through space. They eventually reached Marmola — the marble planet where the crest originated — and rebuilt.

### Berard of Castaka and Epyphite

**Baron Berard of Castaka** — Dayal's descendant — rules Marmola, the world almost entirely of marble. The Castakas quarry it with laser tools, levitate blocks with epyphite, and trade the marble across the galaxy. Epyphite's anti-gravity properties are entirely unknown to the rest of the universe. The Castakas guard the secret for generations. Berard has one daughter, Edna, and it is her marriage to a pirate outsider that begins the Metabaron dynasty.

## Part Two — The Metabaron Dynasty

### Generation Zero — Othon Von Salza and the Founding of the Tradition

**Othon Von Salza** is a pirate with no noble birth. He marries **Edna**, Berard's daughter, and earns his place in the Castaka clan through character rather than lineage. They have one son: **Bari**. The family lives on Marmola, quarrying marble.

One day while cutting marble, Othon is trapped under a massive block in front of dignitaries. The clan votes to save him — revealing the secret of epyphite to the world. Berard, who had disdained Othon for lacking noble birth, acknowledges him as a worthy heir precisely because Othon himself disapproves of the sacrifice that exposed the secret. Berard foresees what will follow, transfers the family's legacy and the Castakan birthmark to Othon, and is cast into the great Azure ocean.

Berard's prediction comes true immediately: hundreds of ships appear over Marmola in a galactic battle for control of epyphite, won by the treacherous black endoguards who turn against the Empire. **Edna dies** in the conflict. Othon is left alone with Bari.

They settle on the planet **Okhar**. A genetically recreated horse named Shazam — extinct for 20,000 years, gifted to Bari by the Empire — is stolen by thieves. Othon, pursuing the thieves in darkness, runs his own son through with his lance. As he lifts the dying boy, the last bandit shoots him, obliterating his groin and hips. In one moment he loses both his son and any capacity to continue the line biologically.

Othon responds not with despair but with invention. He commissions a **multi-protonic pelvis** — the first metabaronic cybernetic implant — and redirects his fortune from martial arts toward developing the first **metabaronic weapons**. The tradition of bodily augmentation as proof of worth begins here, though it is not yet named as such.

He grows cruel and solitary, transforms the rustic Castaka fortress on Okhar into a maxi-protonic tower, living there with the daughters of his servant Iku-Itta.

He earns the title of **Metabaron** by a single audacious act: when pirates disguised as a planet attack the Imperial mother-coach carrying the androgynous siamese twin embryos Janus-Jana, Othon destroys all the pirates and delivers the egg directly to the Emperor and Empress himself, bypassing the escort entirely and thus ensuring no betrayal. The Emperor knights him Metabaron.

#### Honorata

The Empress, as a gift to the castrated warrior, sends a **Shabda-Oud** priestess named **Honorata**. She is magnificent and terrifying — a witch by reputation, a spy by mission. She was sent to produce a specific kind of heir for the Shabda-Oud sisterhood. She chooses differently.

Despite Othon's castration, Honorata impregnates herself with a single drop of his blood. Two jealous servants hurl her from the tower's top. Othon saves her before she hits the ground.

**Aghnar** is born. Epyphite in the birth room makes the baby lighter than air. Othon wants to kill him — the boy floats, which Othon reads as unbattleworthy. Honorata forbids it. She fits steel bracelets around his waist and ankles to keep him earthbound, then takes the child into exile on the sacred mountain of Anasima, a place infested with carnivorous eodactyls and similar horrors. There she trains him for seven years: teaching endurance, forcing him to kill creatures he had befriended to teach control of grief. The day he beats her in combat, she gives him the only kiss he will receive throughout his entire childhood.

When Aghnar turns seven, they return to the fortress. Othon subjects the boy to a formidable robo-killer that grows a new arm every ten minutes, and then to a machine that crushes his feet — a machine he can stop at any time by pressing a button. Aghnar does not press the button. His feet are crushed. He passes. This is the founding moment of the **Metabaronic initiation tradition**: mutilation willingly endured rather than avoided is proof of worth.

### First Generation — Aghnar Von Salza, Great-Grandfather

**Aghnar Von Salza** carries the first generation of accumulated cost in his body: claw-tipped prosthetic hands tipped with epyphite, Oko mini-bombs implanted in his flesh (inherited from his father's body), crushed feet replaced with prosthetics.

#### The Shabda-Oud war and Honorata's sacrifice

Aghnar uses a psy-cloak to infiltrate the Shabda-Oud witches — his mother's former sisterhood, who arranged his own conception as a weapon for their order. He fights them in midair using epyphite on his prosthetic tips. He faces the Mother Superior in a telepathic duel; Honorata links her power to his from a distance and together they destroy her.

Meanwhile Othon's final battle: a Cetacyborg — a cosmic whale-weapon — is sleeping in orbit, programmed to destroy Okhar. Its skin deflects all missiles. Othon flies his Metacraft into the beast's mouth and destroys it from within. The Cetacyborg's intestinal acid destroys his lungs in the process. He is dying.

Then Honorata reveals what she has hidden since the beginning: a micro-H bomb was implanted in her artificial heart by the Shabda-Oud from the start. Surgeon-robots cannot remove it. She cannot travel with those she loves without destroying them. She stays. Othon and Aghnar depart. From space they watch Honorata and all of Okhar annihilate in a nuclear explosion.

They vow revenge on the Shabda-Oud.

On Planet Perdita — dominated by crushing gravity, populated by giant mushrooms, floating apes, and flat predators — Othon is dying from the Cetacyborg's gas. He demands Aghnar kill him in single combat to prove the son capable of avenging Honorata. Othon has implanted Oko mini-bombs throughout his body. Aghnar kills him by piercing his father's brain. He then implants Othon's mini-bombs into his own body.

*The patricide as transfer of power and weapon begins here.*

On Perdita, Aghnar saves a floating ape from predators, injects it with epyphite, and the ape — Kiawoutai — gains speech and a soul. Together they fulfill a prophecy of a small white ape leading them to the Diamond Planet.

#### Oda — and Honorata's second deception

Aghnar's spy-needles locate the Shabda-Oud on an ice asteroid transformed into a comet, guarded by Cetacyborgs. Using a psy-cloak, Aghnar disguises himself as one of the Shabda-Oud witches and attempts to infiltrate the stronghold, using **Oda** — the woman he loves, and whom the witches want — as his pretext for entry. Surrounded by six Cetacyborg guards, he should be annihilated. A force of inestimable power saves them. It is Honorata: alive. She explains that after Othon and Aghnar fled Okhar, she used her Shabda-Oud powers to accelerate time, allowing expert robots to replace her heart-bomb with a new heart made of acturean katriane. She then spent five years building a vessel capable of withstanding nuclear disintegration. Twenty years passed for Honorata; minutes passed for Othon and Aghnar.

Honorata offers to resurrect Oda — who had been killed in the Shabda-Oud fight — using psycho-surgical Shabda-Oud science. She warns the operation will exhaust her near to extinction and require total isolation for years. If her solitude is broken she will die. She also warns: Aghnar and Oda had agreed their child would never become a Metabaron. Aghnar cannot prevent what Honorata has already decided.

The operation succeeds. Oda becomes pregnant. But Oda's behavior shifts immediately afterward — she begins urging Aghnar to make their son the greatest warrior in the universe. Aghnar observes Honorata appearing silently for thirty seconds on the south gallery balcony of the Metabunker each day. Eventually he realizes: Honorata has reincarnated herself inside Oda's body. Oda's soul was destroyed in the Shabda-Oud battle, and Honorata stepped into the vacancy. Tonto had been propping Honorata's corpse on the balcony to maintain the pretense of her survival.

When Aghnar discovers the truth, a catastrophic mental battle erupts — Aghnar versus Honorata inhabiting Oda's body — and the unborn baby joins the fight on the mother's side. A laser-bolt blows off Aghnar's head. Honorata saves him with metabaronic technology.

Aghnar warns Honorata that he will return to duel his son and destroy him if necessary. Honorata accepts this as a renewal of Metabaronic tradition and vows to make the son capable of killing his father. Aghnar enters the Imperial Palace, kills the guards, and publicly renounces service to the Empire — announcing himself henceforth available to the highest bidder. Offers flood in immediately.

#### Aghnar as mercenary

Over twenty years Aghnar sells his services across the galaxy: assisting the Gords against the Hireas, containing a machine revolution for the Zarkots, leading the Trogolosocialik armada, joining pirate gangs, eliminating Schizoprenics for the Emperoress, directing a Morga convoy. He grows weary and old, recognizes his father's aging face in the mirror, contemplates ending his days.

A Pthagurean delegation arrives and offers a fortune to exterminate every living thing within the Empire so they can seize the galaxy. Aghnar accepts. The Empire has no weapon capable of defeating the Metabaron. A Techno-techno ambassador claims to have a solution: a warrior capable of facing him. The holo-image resolves into the features of Oda/Honorata, announcing that the son — Steelhead — is ready.

### Second Generation — Steelhead (Tête d'Acier), Grandfather

**Steelhead** is designated champion of the human galaxy to fight the Metabaron. The duel is arranged with millions of Pthagurean and human spacecraft forming a witnessing ring around their respective Metacrafts. Steelhead traverses inter-dimensional space, battles Aghnar with full metabaronic force. He kills his father. The Pthagureans — whose contract required the Metabaron's death — decree collective suicide. The human galaxy is saved. Steelhead is despised for his methods but effective.

#### The dimensional vampires

The cross-dimensional battle opened a breach in the fabric of the universe. Strange vampires penetrate through walls and devour the brains of celebrants, including the Imperial Couple's. The Emperoress begs Steelhead for help. He agrees, demanding in exchange the title of Supreme Metabaron and the Medallion of High Nobility — requiring unanimous agreement from all planetary ambassadors.

The one dissenter: **Don Nicanor Rosamel de Rokha**, President of the Trogolosocialik Confederation. Steelhead defeats the vampires, reseals the breach, then travels to **Philodendra** — the beautiful natural Trogolosocialik capital — and coldly executes Don Nicanor during his morning meditation at the **Halerce Tree**: the oldest tree in the galaxy, 250,000 years old, inhabited by millions of singing Dyukkas. Steelhead vaporizes Don Nicanor, the Tree, and every Dyukka.

#### Doña Vicenta's public accusation

At the Medallion ceremony, **Doña Vicenta Gabriela de Rokha** — Don Nicanor's daughter — stands before the assembled galaxy and names what Steelhead has done: killed her father, exterminated millions of Dyukkas, and destroyed a living monument 250,000 years old. She casts her vote against the decoration. She announces she will commit suicide immediately after, permanently denying Steelhead the unanimous vote he needs.

Then she makes her most devastating statement: as a machine without a human brain, Steelhead can never know true love. Without love, his line will end. The Metabaron clan will die with him.

Steelhead does not threaten her. He takes this as a challenge. He sets out to find a master who will teach him love.

#### The quest for Zaran Krleza

Steelhead searches the omni-library in the ruins of **Terra Prima** for ancient texts on love. He finds an image of **Zaran Krleza** — the last poet, vanished. Tonto locates coordinates at the galaxy's edge. Steelhead passes through acid rains and a shield of hurricanes to reach him. He takes Krleza's severed head and uses it as a conduit to express tenderness. He combines both identities: warrior and poet. He takes the name **Melmoth**.

He returns to court Doña Vicenta. He wins her heart.

#### Don Nicanor's clone and the crisis

Don Nicanor's clone is produced but missing a portion of memory — he remembers everyone except his own daughter. Locked alone with her, he declares he wants her as his wife and attempts assault. Melmoth intervenes. The clone rallies his people. Doña Vicenta refuses to allow Melmoth to kill her father again or attack the Trogolosocialik; if Melmoth does nothing, the clone will rape her and Melmoth will be destroyed.

**Doña Vicenta's resolution**: she tears her own eyes out with her fingernails and gives them to her father. Don Nicanor — tormented by what his actions have caused — consents to her marriage to Melmoth and his receiving the Medallion of High Nobility.

At the sight of his wife's new cybernetic eyes, Melmoth's personality splits again: Steelhead is pleased she now resembles him; Zaran Krleza's poet-soul is repelled by her appearance.

#### Aghora's birth and Doña Vicenta's death

The Techno-technos bombard their new world. Doña Vicenta develops tuberculosis. She announces she is pregnant — but she is deliberately opening her lungs to the infection. She no longer has a reason to live. Inside her womb are twins: a male child fathered by Steelhead, and a female fathered by Zaran.

Due to complications, only one child can be born. Everyone assumes Steelhead will choose the male heir to perpetuate the clan. He chooses the female child — the poet's daughter. Doña Vicenta, upon hearing this, finds the strength to live long enough to see her daughter born. She suffers a pulmonary hemorrhage and dies.

Steelhead does not grieve in any human way. He saves the female twin by transplanting the male twin's brain into the female body, storing the original male body in a stasis chamber. He names the child **Aghora**. Doña Vicenta's body is preserved in near-death stasis in the Metabunker — Steelhead cannot accept her loss. This preserved corpse will haunt the next generation.

Overcome with grief, Steelhead replaces his own remaining flesh with **Storlon**, an indestructible metal, becoming fully mechanical.

### Third Generation — Aghora, Father-Mother

**Aghora** is the most anomalous figure in the dynasty's history: female-bodied, male-brained, trained as a male Metabaron, and carrying within the same physical form both the poet's daughter and the warrior's son. The dynasty has never had a figure like this. The Bushitaka, designed for father and son, has no grammar for what Aghora is.

#### Upbringing

Steelhead finds a **Tarantuwolf** to nurse Aghora on its milk. By five, Aghora has tamed the beast, naming it Deyanira. The first Metabaronic rite of passage: Aghora's left hand is replaced with a bionic hand, Oko mini-bombs are implanted, and her body is surgically altered so her breasts will never grow — she is trained to live as male. Her first assignment is executioner on the prison planet Khaliyuga, killing criminals from the deadliest species in the galaxy.

#### The impasse — unable to kill Steelhead

By the time Aghora has grown into a full warrior, she must do what the Bushitaka requires: defeat and kill her father in single combat to claim the title of Metabaron. She cannot. Steelhead's body is Storlon — indestructible metal. She cannot penetrate it. The Bushitaka demand is structurally impossible.

Aghora resolves the impasse through two acts of violence-as-mercy: she destroys Doña Vicenta's preserved body — freeing Steelhead from his obsessive grief — and seals Steelhead inside a stasis cocoon, launching him into deep space. She declares the succession complete. It is not, by the letter of the Bushitaka. She hides this from her future son.

#### The birth of Sans-Nom

Aghora births **Sans-Nom** (Nameless) alone — functioning simultaneously as both biological parent and heir-trainer, the sower and the soil. She raises him in the pure tradition of the Metabaronic caste, giving him everything the tradition demands: bodily cost, psychological endurance, weapon mastery, and the crest. She omits one thing: the truth of what she did with Steelhead.

---

### Fourth Generation — Sans-Nom, the Last Metabaron

**Sans-Nom** — Nameless — is the last. He does not name himself. He carries the accumulated weight of five generations in his chest: the crest of Castaka, Aghnar's grief, Steelhead's machine logic, Aghora's divided soul, and the knowledge that the woman who bore him was both his mother and his father.

#### Training and initiation

He develops advanced psionic powers. He is trained to endure physical and psychological pain. He serves as executioner on the same prison planet his father-mother served on. At thirteen his right ear is mutilated. At fourteen, weapons are implanted in his body — including a powerful paralyzing eye-beam. For the next five years he works as a mercenary.

#### The patricide

On the eve of his seventeenth birthday, Sans-Nom kills Aghora. He pierces Aghora's chest as the Bushitaka requires. Before dying, Aghora transfers the Crest of Castaka and everything in it to him. Sans-Nom launches Aghora's coffin into space and triggers Aghora's Oko-bombs. He swears never to have children.

#### Weapons of the Metabaron — the three sacred weapons

Now that Aghora is dead, Tonto reveals the last secret of the Castakas: the location of **Omphale**, at the center of **Enneade**. Inside rest eight sages 300,000 years old, fed by the energy of Ennead. Ennead is surrounded by the main dreamer **Bihargam**, creator of all visions that emerge at night for living beings.

Sans-Nom enters Ennead's dream. He acquires:

1. **Praxis** — a sword with a soul. He faces the Wicked Door, where a Hulzgemeaux attacks. Praxis fills the warrior's heart with the sword's soul, closing the door. The sages warn there are still many Hulzgemeaux in Enneade, three opponents to overcome, and two more weapons to find.

2. **The omnigrail** — held by a tribe of cannibal lizard-men on the planet Bottra. Sans-Nom frees aboriginal child prisoners, destroys the lizard-men's temple with his Superkolt, defeats a giant spider using Praxis, and acquires the omnigrail.

3. **The transpinal eye** — a mini-computer with its own will that escaped into the concentric rings of Trafalhar. Retrieved after defeating a formless-matter monster (using Praxis vibration), fire-breathing dragons hatched from egg-asteroids (destroyed by Oko-missile targeting the brain-asteroid), and a colony of leeches.

#### Steelhead's return and Lothar's revelation

Steelhead — ejected into deep space by Aghora — drifts until swallowed by a **Trans-ludens**, a dimensional creature that transports him to Ulkmar-eight-moon, the most sacred planet in the galaxy. Unable to digest Steelhead's Storlon body, the Trans-ludens defecates him on the volcano Hungar. Local Proto-anthropoids worship him as the divine fetus Ounser and bleed their bosoms to honor him. Once freed, Steelhead eliminates all the tribes of the planet, waits years for the Trans-ludens to return, forces it to swallow him again, and rides it back to his own galaxy.

At Terra 2014, when Sans-Nom is thirty years old, Steelhead arrives and demands mortal combat for the title of Metabaron.

Sans-Nom allows Steelhead to attack first — but has measured the space between them with such precision that only his right eyebrow is split. The scar. He then paralyzes Steelhead with his eye-beam. He opens Steelhead's pectoral brain, removes portions of the lobular circuits, erases his memory, and encases Steelhead's indestructible body inside a heavy metallic armor. He installs him in the Metabunker as Lothar — the companion robot who has been listening to Tonto tell this entire story.

#### The supra-lice and the final battle

The supra-lice — a galactic threat — erupt. A space battle is fought involving Steelhead and the Metabaron. Sans-Nom ultimately prevails. Steelhead sacrifices himself to destroy **Diophante**, giving his grandson something clean to stand on.

#### The Metabaron's declaration

Sans-Nom decides to cleanse evil from the universe. He destroys the government headquarters of the Golden Planet and plans to confront the imperial powers, the Techno-techno cult, the magnates, the ekonomats, and the colonial forces throughout the galaxy — restoring freedom. He sterilizes himself. The dynasty ends by his own decision: not through death, not through failure, but through deliberate choice.

## The Bushitaka Code

The Bushitaka is the governing law of the Metabaron dynasty. It operates at every level: succession, burial, honor, war, and self-definition.

**Core tenets:**

- The title of Metabaron passes from father to son (or equivalent) through mortal combat. The heir must kill the titleholder. The titleholder must die by their own successor's hand.
- The initiation requires a witnessed, irreversible bodily cost — mutilation endured or transformation undergone as proof of worth. Each generation's cost is different: Othon's pelvis, Aghnar's crushed feet and prosthetic claws, Steelhead's flesh replaced entirely with Storlon metal after Vicenta's death, Aghora's left hand replaced and body surgically altered, Sans-Nom's right ear destroyed at thirteen.
- The dead must be buried. When burial on solid ground is impossible — as on Marmola's marble surface — alternative rites must be found.
- Refusal of the Bushitaka is possible but catastrophic. Aghnar tried to protect his son from it; Honorata overrode him. Aghora could not complete it and hid the failure. Each evasion costs the next generation.
- The Bushitaka governs public consequence: power is proved through visible, witnessed cost, not private intention or internal virtue.

**What the Bushitaka cannot account for:**
- Honorata's reincarnation (she died and returned through another's body)
- Steelhead's indestructible Storlon body (Aghora cannot complete the killing stroke)
- Aghora's dual gender (the law has no grammar for Father-Mother)
- Sans-Nom's decision to end the line voluntarily (the code has no clause for deliberate extinction)

Each of these impossibilities exposes the code's limits — and each generation, in trying to work around the limit, transmits a wound to the next.

## Key Concepts and Technology

### Epyphite
A sacred oil with anti-gravity properties, unknown to the rest of the universe, discovered by the Castaka clan under Marmola's marble surface. Source of the clan's wealth and the cause of their destruction (the galactic war over epyphite kills Edna and triggers the battle over Marmola). Also used practically: Aghnar tips his prosthetic claws with it to fight in midair against the Shabda-Oud; at Aghnar's birth, epyphite in the delivery room makes the newborn lighter than air; injected into the Magon ape Kiawoutai, it gives the creature speech and a soul.

### The Crest of Castaka
A living mark burned into the ruler's chest, transferred from parent to child at the moment of succession. It carries the accumulated memories, knowledge, and spiritual presence of all prior ancestors. The holder can draw on this inherited wisdom. It is the dynasty's most sacred and most intimate inheritance — it cannot be seized, only given, and only in the act of dying.

### The Metabunker
The Metabaron's mobile fortress and home. A massive, self-contained spacecraft capable of full-scale combat. It is destroyed and rebuilt at least once during the saga (obliterated by the Metabaron himself when enemies threaten to discover his one weakness; Tonto and Lothar construct a new one on a primitive muddy planet). The Metabunker's internal life — the waiting, the stories told, the accidental activations of the bio-electrogram — is the frame through which the entire dynasty is narrated.

### The Metacraft
The Metabaron's personal combat vessel. Extremely advanced, capable of cross-dimensional travel. Othon crashes his into the Cetacyborg's mouth to destroy it. The metacraft is central to the formal duels between generations.

### The Bio-electrogram
A solid-state holographic projection of the Metabaron that lasts only three minutes before dissolving. It retains combat capabilities. It accidentally destroys the Metabunker when Lothar activates it carelessly. In Vol. 4, when Lothar asks the bio-electrogram about the scar, the Metabaron shoots him before dissolving.

### Oko Mini-Bombs
Small explosive devices implanted in the Metabaron's body, passed down the generations. Othon implanted them throughout his own body; when Aghnar killed him, Aghnar physically extracted and implanted his father's bombs into his own body. Subsequent generations inherit the bombs as part of the full succession — Sans-Nom triggers Aghora's Oko-bombs after the patricide, ejecting her coffin into space.

### The Superkolt
The Metabaron's personal sidearm: an enormously powerful handheld weapon capable of destroying fortresses and killing armadas at close range.

### Praxis
A sword with a soul, acquired from the sages of Omphale inside the Ennead dream. It functions as a spiritual amplifier: it fills the warrior's heart with its soul, allowing him to face entities that cannot be defeated by force alone. It is one of three sacred weapons the last Metabaron must acquire.

### Storlon
An indestructible metal that Steelhead replaces his flesh with after Doña Vicenta's death. It makes him physically invulnerable — and structurally prevents Aghora from completing the succession rite. Lothar's heavy metallic armor in the framing device is the outer shell over Steelhead's Storlon body.

## Factions and Powers

### The Empire / The Emperoress Janus-Jana
The galactic empire is ruled by androgynous siamese twin imperial embryos — **Janus-Jana** — created from the Emperor's and Empress's genetic material. The Metabaron dynasty has served the Empire for generations, but the relationship is transactional and strained. Aghnar publicly renounces imperial service and markets himself as a mercenary. The Empire uses Steelhead against the dimensional vampires but demands impossible political costs (the unanimous vote) in exchange. By Sans-Nom's generation, the Empire is among the galactic powers he intends to confront.

### The Shabda-Oud
An order of psychic witches who arranged Honorata's mission from the beginning: she was to produce a hermaphrodite heir (combining male and female power in one body) for the sisterhood's purposes. Honorata chose her own direction instead and bore a male son. The sisterhood remains antagonistic across multiple generations — their Mother Superior is killed by Aghnar and Honorata together; their Cetacyborg weapons are used as guards; they are the force whose actions shattered Oda's soul in the conflict on the ice comet asteroid — leaving her body a shell that Honorata then occupied. The hermaphrodite heir they demanded eventually arrives anyway, five generations later, through Steelhead's unexpected choice: Aghora is the female-bodied, male-brained child they wanted — delivered by love rather than by their scheming.

### The Techno-Technos
A technoguild cult that worships technology and operates through the Techno-pope. They destroyed the planet Ahour to clear orbital space for their artificial world, infecting the king with paleo-tuberculosis to trigger political cover. They bombard Steelhead and Doña Vicenta repeatedly. They serve as a persistent imperial-adjacent threat throughout the saga. Sans-Nom names them among the powers he will confront in his final declaration.

### The Pthagureans
An alien species seeking total galactic conquest. They hire Aghnar to exterminate all life within the Empire — a contract he accepts after his mercenary years leave him weary and purposeless. When Steelhead kills Aghnar, the Pthagureans honor their agreement and decree collective suicide, ending their own threat simultaneously.

### The Trogolosocialik Confederation
Don Nicanor's political body. The Trogolosocialik capital Philodendra is a planet of extraordinary natural beauty, destroyed by Steelhead when he vaporizes the Halerce Tree and its Dyukkas to eliminate the dissenting vote. Doña Vicenta — Don Nicanor's daughter — is the last significant Trogolosocialik character and the moral center of Steelhead's arc.

### The Ekonomats and Magnates
Named in Sans-Nom's final declaration as among the galactic powers he will confront. They represent the financial-colonial infrastructure of the empire, never depicted directly in the main saga but named as targets of the last Metabaron's promised purge.

## Cosmic and Mystical Elements

### The Omphale and the Ennead
At the center of the galaxy exists **Enneade**, a realm where eight sages 300,000 years old rest in a sleeping room, fed by Ennead's energy. The entire structure is surrounded by **Bihargam**, the main dreamer — a primordial entity who generates all the visions that visit living beings during sleep. The Metabaron enters this dream-space to acquire his three sacred weapons. The location of Omphale is the last secret of the Castakas, held by Tonto and revealed only after Aghora's death.

### Trans-ludens
A dimensional creature capable of traveling between galaxies. It swallowed Steelhead's ejected stasis sphere and transported him to Ulkmar-eight-moon. Unable to digest Storlon, it deposited him. Steelhead then forced the creature to carry him back.

### Cetacyborgs
Cosmic whale-creatures, genetically and mechanically weaponized. Their outer skin deflects all missiles. Their intestinal acid corrodes biological matter. They are used by the Shabda-Oud as guards and weapons. Othon destroys one from inside; others are referenced as fleet-scale threats.

### The Dimensional Vampires
Entities that enter the material universe through cross-dimensional breaches — in this case, the breach torn open by Steelhead and Aghnar's interdimensional duel. They penetrate walls and devour brains. Steelhead defeats them and reseals the breach as his price for the Medallion of High Nobility.

### The Hulzgemeaux
Entities encountered inside the Ennead dream-space. They represent spiritual obstacles that cannot be defeated by physical force alone. Praxis enables the warrior to face them by filling his heart with the sword's soul.

### The Magon Apes
Large floating apes on Planet Perdita. Normally wordless animals, they gain speech and soul when injected with epyphite. Kiawoutai — the ape Aghnar saves and elevates — becomes his companion. They fulfill a prophecy of a small white ape leading the Magons to the Diamond Planet.

## The Scar — the Saga's Central Mystery

The entire framing device is organized around one question: how did the Metabaron get the scar above his right eyebrow?

Tonto uses it as the story's spine: Lothar asks, Tonto begins telling, across eight volumes and multiple centuries, the complete history of the dynasty, to arrive at the moment when Steelhead's first strike grazes only the eyebrow because Sans-Nom has measured the distance precisely. The scar is proof of a single thing: the Metabaron is not invincible. He allowed himself to receive the scar to demonstrate that he had measured the encounter perfectly — that his survival was calculation, not luck, and that even an ancestor in an indestructible body could be neutralized without destruction.

It is also proof that the Metabaron is not a myth. He bled once. The scar is the evidence.

## Thematic Architecture

### Power proven through visible cost
The dynasty's foundational principle is that legitimacy is not claimed — it is demonstrated through irreversible, witnessed bodily sacrifice. Othon's pelvis. Aghnar's feet. Steelhead's flesh replaced with indestructible metal. Aghora's surgically altered body. Sans-Nom's ear. The costs escalate and compound across generations. Private virtue, good intentions, and internal resolve mean nothing; what matters is what the witness can see and cannot unsee.

### The love that breaks the machine and the machine that breaks the love
Every Metabaron falls in love. Every love costs them something that cannot be recovered. Othon loses Edna to the epyphite war. Aghnar loses Oda to the Shabda-Oud — and then discovers the thing he loved was replaced before he could grieve it. Steelhead builds a human soul to deserve Vicenta and then loses her to tuberculosis and his own inability to stop being a weapon. Aghora is both parents to Sans-Nom and loves him fully — then dies at his hands as the law requires. Love is not sentimental decoration in this saga. It is the force that most consistently undoes the warrior and the force that most consistently drives the dynasty forward.

### The failed escape
Every generation tries to exit the Bushitaka's logic: Aghnar promises his child will never be a Metabaron; Honorata overrides him. Aghora cannot kill Steelhead and hides this; Sans-Nom carries an illegitimate title and makes peace with it. Vicenta tries to change Steelhead through love and partly succeeds — and partly fails. The exits that actually work come not from refusal inside the system but from completing it so thoroughly that there is nothing left to pass on. Sans-Nom is the only one who exits cleanly, because he decides from inside the finished machine rather than fighting it from within an incomplete one.

### Witnesses and memory
The dynasty's legitimacy depends entirely on who watches and what they remember. Tonto is the memory-keeper — a robot whose function is to narrate, accurately and at length, what happened and why. The Crest carries the ancestors' witness from the inside. The bio-electrogram preserves the Metabaron's combat record. The scar on the eyebrow is a permanent public record of one specific moment. Servants, companions, rivals, and bystanders are not scenery; they are the archive. Without witnesses, cost has no meaning.

### The hermaphrodite ideal
The Shabda-Oud wanted a hermaphrodite heir from the beginning — a figure combining male and female power in one body. Honorata refused and bore a male son. Steelhead made a single unexpected choice — the female twin, the poet's daughter — and Aghora arrived: male-brained, female-bodied, both warrior and nurturer, executioner and parent. The sisterhood's design came true despite every effort to prevent it. This is Jodorowsky's recurring interest in androgyny as a form of completeness: the dynasty's most fully realized figure is the one who contains both halves.

## Timeline Orientation

The saga spans approximately four to five centuries of dynasty time, though Tonto and Lothar age very little within the framing device — robots, not humans. Key temporal markers:

- **Centuries before Vol. 1**: Dayal and the Castaka on Ahour. Epyphite discovered on Marmola.
- **Vol. 1 era**: Othon founds the tradition. Edna dies. Aghnar born.
- **Three centuries later** (per Vol. 2 framing): City Shaft is deserted; Tonto narrates the Honorata story.
- **Aghnar's arc**: Twenty years of mercenary work after renouncing imperial service.
- **Vol. 5–6**: Steelhead's arc — the duel, the quest for Krleza, the twin pregnancy, Vicenta's death.
- **Aghora's arc**: childhood through Sans-Nom's birth.
- **Sans-Nom's arc**: Birth → age 17 (patricide) → age 30 (Steelhead duel) → final declaration.
- **Framing device**: Tonto and Lothar have waited "three months" for the Metabaron's return at the saga's opening; this expands metaphorically across the centuries of the story itself. By Vol. 6, City Shaft is "just a chunk of planet floating in space, the last fragment of a disintegrated galaxy."
