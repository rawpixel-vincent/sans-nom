# The Metabarons — Comic Summaries

---

## 1. Othon the Great-Great-Grandfather
**Issue #1 of *The Saga of the Metabarons* | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Juan Giménez | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 1992**
*English editions: "The Stonecutters" and "The Last Stand" (2000)*

**Framing device:** In City Shaft, the robot Tonto and his companion Lothar have been waiting three months for the return of their master, the Metabaron. To pass the time, Tonto explains the initiatory tradition of the meta-warriors — why their master has a bionic ear and lobe: as a child, his right ear and part of his brain were destroyed by his own father as part of the initiation, just as his grandfather had cut off his father's left hand. Lothar becomes intrigued and asks Tonto to narrate the full history of the metabaron caste.

**Flashback — Othon's origins:** The story begins with Othon Von Salza, a pirate who has married Edna, the daughter of Baron Berard of Castaka, ruler of the planet Marmola — a world made almost entirely of marble. They live without robot assistance, using only prehistoric tools for defense. The Castaka clan trades precious marble, cutting blocks with laser-tools and levitating them with *epyphite*, a sacred oil with anti-gravity powers unknown to the rest of the universe. They have one son, Bari.

**The revelation of epyphite:** One day while cutting marble, Othon becomes trapped beneath a massive block in front of dignitaries. The clan votes to save him, thereby revealing the secret of the epyphite that has been theirs for generations. Baron Berard, who had initially disdained Othon for lacking noble birth, acknowledges him as a worthy successor when he sees that Othon himself disapproves of the sacrifice. Before dying, Berard foresees disaster and transfers the family's legacy and the Castakan birthmark to Othon, then is cast into the great Azure ocean.

**Galactic war:** Berard's prediction immediately comes true. Hundreds of ships appear over Marmola in a terrible space battle for control of the epyphite, won by the treacherous black endoguards who turn against the Empire.

**Edna's death and Othon's castration:** The conflict claims Edna's life. Othon is left alone with Bari on the planet Okhar. There Bari receives a magnificent gift: a horse named Shazam, extinct for 20,000 years and genetically recreated by the Empire's best scientists. Their peaceful days are short-lived — the horse is stolen by thieves, and Othon, mistaking Bari for one of them, runs his own son through with his lance. As he lifts the dying boy, the last bandit shoots him, obliterating his groin and hips. Servants cannot restore his manhood. In a single moment Othon has lost both his son and any hope of continuing the clan.

**Foundation of the metabaronic tradition:** Othon turns his back on pure martial arts and invests much of his fortune in developing the first metabaronic weapons. He also begins the tradition of cybernetic implants by incorporating a multi-protonic pelvis into his own body.

## 2. Honorata the Great-Great-Grandmother
**Issue #2 of *The Saga of the Metabarons* | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Juan Giménez | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 1993**
*English editions: "The Knighting of Othon" and "Honorata, the Sorceress" (2000)*

**Framing device:** The story has now skipped three centuries. City Shaft is deserted, with only cyber-cops on watch. Suddenly the last Zacuda Frolex in existence drops through Police Central's ventilation hatches, lays a single egg before dying, and a mutant Zacuda Frolex hatches — accidentally reactivating the attack program in the Central Brain, sending cyber-cops to assault the Metabunker. Tonto and Lothar activate the Metabaron's bio-electrogram (which lasts only three minutes in solid state before dissolving) to defend themselves; it uses the metacraft to destroy the cyber-cops. With the danger past, Tonto continues the story.

**Flashback — Othon's transformation:** The castrated Othon has grown cruel and solitary. He sacrifices the natural beauty of Okhar, transforming the rustic Castaka fortress into a maxi-protonic tower, living there with the two daughters of his faithful servant Iku-Itta.

**The Imperial egg:** Scientists announce the creation of androgynous siamese twin embryos from the Emperor's and Empress's genetic material — the being called Janus-Jana. A horde of pirates disguised as a planet attacks the Mother-coach carrying the Imperial offspring. The Emperor calls for help. Othon uses advanced technology and ruthless bombardment to wipe out all the pirates, then takes the Imperial egg directly to its parents himself, rather than joining the escort — ensuring no betrayal. For this, the Emperor knights Othon as Metabaron.

**Honorata:** The Empress promises to scour the galaxy for a gift to fulfill the castrated warrior. One night a Shabda-Oud priestess named Honorata arrives on Okhar, sent by the Empress. Despite her reputation as a witch she is magnificent and desirable, and entrances Othon. Although Othon can no longer father a child, Honorata impregnates herself with a single drop of his blood. Her pregnancy proceeds normally, but two jealous servants hurl her from the top of the fortress. Othon saves her before she hits the ground.

**Birth of Aghnar:** The introduction of epyphite during the birth makes baby Aghnar lighter than air. Othon wants to kill the boy, deeming him unfit to be a warrior, but Honorata — now sterile — forbids it. She places steel bracelets around his waist and ankles to keep him on the ground, then goes into exile on the sacred mountain of Anasima, filled with monsters like carnivorous eodactyls, where she teaches her son to use his mental powers and resist pain, even forcing him to kill creatures he had befriended to teach control of grief. The day Aghnar manages to beat her in combat, she gives him the only kiss he will receive throughout his entire childhood.

**Aghnar's trials:** When Aghnar turns seven, they return to the Castaka fortress. Othon makes the boy fight a formidable robo-killer that grows a new arm every ten minutes. Then he subjects Aghnar to a machine that crushes his feet — a machine the boy can stop at any time. Aghnar does not budge. His feet are crushed, but he passes the test. Tonto notes that this is how mutilation and prostheses became key ingredients of the Metabaronic initiation.

## 3. Aghnar the Great-Grandfather
**Issue #3 of *The Saga of the Metabarons* | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Juan Giménez | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 1995**
*English editions: "The Snare of Okhar" and "The Trials of Aghnar" (2000)*

**Framing device:** Tonto is making breakfast for the Metabaron despite his continued absence. Lothar accidentally activates the Metabaron's bio-electrogram, which slices the Metabunker in half. Tonto and Lothar use ant-robots to re-unite the two halves. Lothar notes that the Metabaron has a scar on his right eyebrow — proof he is not invincible. Tonto resumes the story.

**Flashback — Confronting the Shabda-Oud:** Using a psy-cloak, Aghnar disguises himself as one of the Shabda-Oud witches and infiltrates their order, pretending to deliver a captive. When the witches begin to uncover the deception, Aghnar attacks with his claw-tipped prostheses. The prosthetics are tipped with epyphite, allowing him to overcome the assassins in midair. He then faces the Mother Superior in a telepathic duel. Honorata links her power to her son's, and together they obliterate the Mother Superior.

**Othon's death:** Othon climbs into his Metacraft to destroy the sleeping Cetacyborg — a cosmic whale programmed to destroy Okhar. Its outer skin repels all missiles, so Othon plunges the Metacraft into the beast's mouth and destroys it from the inside. However, the Cetacyborg's intestinal acid corrodes his lungs in the process. To evade reprisals from the Shabda-Oud, they flee Okhar aboard the Metabunker. Before leaving, Honorata reveals that a micro-H bomb has been implanted in her from the beginning — her own artificial heart is the bomb, and surgeon-robots cannot remove it. She cannot leave with those she loves without destroying them. Father and son depart and watch from space as Honorata and all of Okhar are annihilated in a nuclear explosion. They vow revenge.

**Planet Perdita:** Othon and Aghnar take refuge on Planet Perdita, dominated by powerful gravity. Giant mushrooms are inhabited by floating apes; the surface crawls with flat voracious animals that wait for the occasional ape that falls like ripe fruit. Othon dispatches spy-needles into space with instructions to locate the Shabda-Oud within ten years. But Othon is dying from the Cetacyborg's gasses. He demands Aghnar kill him in single combat to ensure the son is capable of avenging Honorata. Othon has implanted his body with Oko mini-bombs. Aghnar kills him by piercing his father's brain, then implants his father's mini-bombs into his own body.

**The Magon apes:** Aghnar saves a floating ape from the flat land predators and injects it with epyphite. The ape — a Magon named Kiawoutai — can now speak and has a soul. Together they fly to the tops of the mushrooms, where Aghnar confronts and defeats the Magon leader, fulfilling a prophecy foretelling of a small white ape who would lead them to the Diamond Planet.

**Discovery of Oda's whereabouts:** Aghnar's spy-needles return with news: the Shabda-Oud are living on an ice asteroid transformed into a comet, guarded by Cetacyborgs. Aghnar also learns of Oda.

## 4. Oda the Great-Grandmother
**Issue #4 of *The Saga of the Metabarons* | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Juan Giménez | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 1997**
*English editions: "The Lair of the Shabda-Oud," "The Possession of Oda," and "The Mentrek's Solution" (2000)*

**Framing device:** Lothar asks the bio-electrogram of the Metabaron how he got the scar; the Metabaron shoots Lothar before dissolving. Tonto repairs Lothar with ant-robots and resumes Aghnar's story.

**Flashback — Infiltrating the Shabda-Oud:** Using a psy-cloak, Aghnar disguises himself as one of the Shabda-Oud witches and pretends to deliver Oda, whom the witches want. Despite being surrounded by six Cetacyborg guards, Aghnar escapes back to the Metabunker through a mysterious force of inestimable power. Their savior reveals herself: it is Honorata. She explains that after Othon and Aghnar fled Okhar, she used her Shabda-Oud powers to speed up time so that expert robots could replace her heart-bomb with a new heart made of acturean katriane. She then spent five years building a vessel capable of withstanding the nuclear disintegration of the planet. While twenty years passed for her on Okhar, only minutes passed for Othon and Aghnar as they fled.

**Oda's resurrection:** Honorata offers to restore Oda to life using the psycho-surgical science of the Shabda-Oud. Aghnar cautions that he and Oda had agreed their child would never become a Metabaron. Honorata warns that the operation will exhaust her near to extinction for several years, requiring total isolation — if her solitude is broken she will die. Aghnar agrees. The operation succeeds and Oda becomes pregnant.

**Honorata's possession of Oda:** During the pregnancy, Oda's attitude shifts dramatically — she tries to persuade Aghnar to make their son the greatest warrior in the universe. Meanwhile Aghnar observes Honorata from a distance as she appears silently for 30 seconds on the south gallery balcony of the Metabunker. It is eventually revealed that Honorata has reincarnated herself inside Oda's body (Tonto had been bringing Honorata's corpse to the balcony to maintain the pretense she was still alive). When Aghnar realizes the truth, a terrible mental battle erupts between them; the unborn baby joins the fight on the mother's side. A laser-bolt blows off Aghnar's head, but Honorata saves him using metabaronic technology.

**The son's birth and Aghnar's declaration:** Aghnar warns Honorata that he will return to duel his son and destroy him. Honorata accepts this as a renewal of Metabaronic tradition and vows to make her son a champion capable of killing his father. Aghnar then enters the Imperial Palace, slaughters the guards, and declares to the Emperoress Janus-Jana that he is discontinuing his service to the Empire, henceforth offering himself as a mercenary to the highest bidder — which immediately triggers an avalanche of offers.

**Aghnar's mercenary years:** Over twenty years Aghnar sells his services to many causes: assisting the Gords against the Hireas, serving the Zarkots to contain a machine revolution, leading the Trogolosocialik armada, joining the red pirate gang of Ulritch, eliminating Schizoprenics under contract to the Emperoress, and directing a convoy of Morga. Eventually, sad and weary in his secret lair on Anasirma — the last remains of his home planet — and recognizing his father's aging traits in the mirror, he contemplates ending his days. A Pthagurean delegation arrives and offers a fortune to exterminate every living thing within the Empire, so they can take over the galaxy. Aghnar accepts. The Empire has no weapon capable of defeating the Metabaron. A Techno-techno ambassador claims to hold a solution: a warrior capable of facing Aghnar. The holo-image breaks apart to reveal the features of Oda/Honorata, announcing that his son — Steelhead — is ready.

## 5. Steelhead the Grandfather
**Issue #5 of *The Saga of the Metabarons* | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Juan Giménez | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 1998**
*English editions: "The Clash of the Meta-Warriors," "Steelhead's Quest," and "Melmoth's Plight" (2001)*

**Framing device:** Tonto and Lothar have now been waiting centuries; City Shaft is empty. They test the Metabaron's bio-electrogram by having it fight 180 cerbomaks. Then Tonto continues.

**Flashback — Steelhead appointed champion:** Steelhead is appointed champion of the human galaxy to fight the Metabaron. A duel is arranged, with millions of Pthagurean and human spacecraft forming a circle around their respective Metacrafts. Steelhead battles Aghnar, kills him, and is declared champion. Even if despised for his methods, he has saved the human race; the Pthagureans decree collective suicide.

**The dimensional vampires:** While soldiers celebrate, strange vampires penetrate through walls and devour the brains of the guests, including the Imperial Couple's. The Emperoress begs Steelhead for help: these entities entered the universe through a breach opened by his cross-dimensional battle with Aghnar. Steelhead agrees, demanding in exchange the title of Supreme Metabaron and the Medallion of High Nobility — which requires agreement from all planetary ambassadors. To obtain this, he must eliminate the one figure who would vote against it: Don Nicanor Rosamel de Rokha, President of the Trogolosocialik Confederation. Steelhead defeats the vampires, reseals the breach, then travels to Philodendra — the natural, beauteous Trogolosocialik capital — and coldly executes Don Nicanor during his morning meditation at the Halerce Tree, the oldest tree in the galaxy, vaporizing both it and the millions of Dyukkas that sing within it.

**Doña Vicenta's challenge:** As Steelhead receives the Medallion of High Nobility, Don Nicanor's daughter Doña Vicenta accuses him of killing her father, exterminating millions of Dyukkas, and destroying the 250,000-year-old Halerce Tree. She casts her vote against the decoration and announces she will commit suicide immediately afterward — permanently depriving Steelhead of the award. Her words wound him deeply. Most critically, she predicts the end of the Metabaron clan: as a machine without a human brain, he can never know true love. Steelhead takes this as a challenge and sets out to find a master who will teach him love.

**The quest for Zaran Krleza:** Steelhead searches the omni-library in the ruins of Terra Prima for ancient texts on love, without success, until he finds an image of Zaran Krleza — the last poet, long disappeared. After Tonto locates his coordinates at the galaxy's edge, Steelhead passes through acid rains and a shield of hurricanes to reach him. He takes Krleza's head and uses it as a conduit to express tenderness.

**Melmoth:** Steelhead takes the name Melmoth — combining the warrior and the poet — and returns to court Doña Vicenta. He wins her heart.

**Don Nicanor's clone:** Don Nicanor's clone is produced but has lost a portion of his memory — he remembers everyone except his own daughter. Shutting himself alone with her, he declares he wants her as his wife and attempts to assault her. Melmoth intervenes to separate them. The President rallies his people. Doña Vicenta refuses to allow Melmoth to kill her father again or attack her people, yet if Melmoth does nothing, he will be destroyed and Don Nicanor will rape her.

## 6. Doña Vicenta Gabriela de Rokha the Grandmother
**Issue #6 of *The Saga of the Metabarons* | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Juan Giménez | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 1999**
*English editions: "Melmoth's Plight," "The Torment of Doña Vicenta," and "Galactic Threat" (2001)*

**Framing device:** Back in the Metabunker, Tonto refuses to tell Lothar any more stories. Overwhelmed by depression, and by the fact that their master has been absent so long, Lothar begins throwing himself against the walls in a suicide attempt. The Metabaron himself then suddenly appears — but tells the robots he has returned only to disintegrate them and blow up the Metabunker, so that his enemies cannot discover the secret of his scar, his only weakness. He shows them that City Shaft is now just a chunk of planet floating in space, the last fragment of a disintegrated galaxy. Before the robots are destroyed, Tonto asks to be consumed in the nuclear explosion instead, for the sake of their honor. The Metabaron speeds away as his fortress explodes. Tonto and Lothar are left on a primitive, muddy planet. They begin constructing a new Metabunker and continue the story to pass the time.

**Flashback — Don Nicanor's resolution:** Don Nicanor and his Philodendran army storm the room where Melmoth and Doña Vicenta are barricaded. He declares that what he loves most about her are her eyes — so Doña Vicenta tears her own eyes out with her fingernails and gives them to him. Tormented by what his actions have caused, Don Nicanor consents to her marriage to Melmoth and to his receiving the Medallion of High Nobility.

**Melmoth's divided personality:** At the sight of his wife's new cybernetic eyes, Melmoth's personality splits again into its two components. Steelhead is pleased that his wife now resembles him; Zaran is repelled by her appearance.

**The Techno-techno attack:** The Techno-technos bombard the planet. On the new, artificial world they move to, Doña Vicenta develops tuberculosis, and announces she is pregnant. Steelhead discovers she is deliberately opening her lungs to the infection — she no longer has any reason to live. Inside her womb are twins: a male child fathered by Steelhead, and a female fathered by Zaran.

**The choice:** Tonto informs them that due to complications only one of the babies can be born. Doña Vicenta bitterly assumes Steelhead will save the male child to perpetuate his clan. When Steelhead asks her what would happen if he chose to save the girl, Doña Vicenta replies that she would find the strength to live again, to raise the child born of the man she loved. Tonto ends his tale with the revelation that Steelhead does indeed choose the female child — leaving Lothar wondering how the tradition of the Metabarons, which can only pass from father to son, could possibly be preserved.

## 7. Aghora the Father-Mother
**Issue #7 of *The Saga of the Metabarons* | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Juan Giménez | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 2002**
*English editions: "Aghora, the Father-Mother," "The Mirror Effect," "The Return of the Shabda-Oud"*

**Birth and the siamese solution:** Near death due to tuberculosis, Doña Vicenta can only save one of the twins. Steelhead surprises her by choosing the female child. He saves the siamese twins by transplanting the boy's brain into the girl's body, storing the boy's body in a stasis chamber. The child is named Aghora. Just as Doña Vicenta sees her daughter, she suffers a pulmonary hemorrhage. Steelhead preserves her in a state of near-death.

**Aghora's upbringing:** Steelhead finds a Tarantuwolf to nurse Aghora on its milk. By the time Aghora is five years old she tames the great beast, naming it Deyanira. During the first Metabaronic rite of passage, Aghora's left hand is replaced with a bionic hand, Oko mini-bombs are implanted, and her body is altered so her breasts will never grow and she can live as a male. Aghora's first assignment is to serve as executioner on the prison planet Khaliyuga, killing criminals from the deadliest species in the galaxy.

**Steelhead's grief:** Overcome with grief for Doña Vicenta, Steelhead replaces his own flesh with Storlon, an indestructible metal.

**Aghora unable to defeat Steelhead:** Unable to slay her father in combat for the title of Metabaron, Aghora destroys Doña Vicenta's body and seals Steelhead in a stasis cocoon, launching him into deep space. For three years Aghora waits for the Imperial Court to grant her an assignment, but no summons ever arrives.

**Aghora's subsequent career and the birth of Nameless:** The story continues with Aghora's struggles as a Father-Mother figure who eventually gives birth to Nameless.

## 8. Nameless, the Last of the Metabarons
**Issue #8 of *The Saga of the Metabarons* | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Juan Giménez | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 2003**

**Framing device:** Lothar is angry at Tonto for humiliating him. Tonto explains his taunting by recounting how the Metabaron got the scar on his right eyebrow. Tonto first reminds Lothar that Aghora actually broke the Bushitaka law — she did not truly kill Steelhead, she simply launched him into space.

**Flashback — Nameless's upbringing:** After Aghora birthed Nameless, the boy developed advanced psionic powers, was taught to endure physical and psychological pain, and served as an executioner on a prison planet. At age 13 his ear was mutilated; at 14 weapons were implanted in his body; for the next five years he worked as a mercenary.

**The patricide:** On the eve of his 17th birthday, Nameless faced the Metabaronic tradition: he had to kill his father-mother to claim the title of Metabaron. Nameless pierces Aghora's chest, killing him/her. Afterward, Nameless swears never to have children.

**Steelhead's long journey:** Meanwhile, Steelhead — sealed in his invulnerable sphere — drifted through space until swallowed by a Trans-ludens, which transported him to Ulkmar-eight-moon, the most sacred planet in the galaxy. Unable to digest Steelhead, the Trans-ludens defecated him on the slopes of the volcano Hungar. Local Proto-anthropoids, convinced Steelhead was the divine fetus Ounser, honored him by drinking blood from their own bosoms. Once freed, Steelhead eliminated all the tribes of the planet, then waited years for the Trans-ludens to return, allowed himself to be swallowed again, and forced the creature to carry him back to his own galaxy.

**The duel and Lothar's true identity:** Steelhead arrives at Terra 2014 when Nameless is 30 years old and challenges him to a mortal combat for the title of Metabaron. Nameless provokes Steelhead into attacking but has measured the space between them with such precision that only his right eyebrow is split in two — the origin of the scar. Nameless then eye-blasts Steelhead with a powerful paralyzing ray. Taking advantage of his ancestor's catatonia, Nameless opens Steelhead's pectoral brain, removes portions of the lobular circuits, erases his memory, and makes him believe he is a robot — fitting Steelhead's indestructible body inside a heavy metallic armor. This is what Lothar has believed himself to be all along. Tonto's tale thus reveals that Lothar is in fact Steelhead.

**The supra-lice and the final battle:** The supra-lice threaten the universe. A space battle erupts between Steelhead and the Metabaron. The Metabaron ultimately prevails.

**The Metabaron's declaration:** The Metabaron decides to return to Galaxia Prima and begin cleansing evil from the universe. He destroys the government headquarters of the Golden Planet and plans to restore freedom throughout the galaxy by confronting the imperial powers, the Techno-techno cult, the magnates, the ekonomats, and the colonial forces.

## 9. The Crest of Castaka
**Short story | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Juan Giménez | Released 2000**
*(Published in the special volume "The House of the Ancestors." This story would later serve as the basis for the Castaka series and is retold in its entirety in The Rival Twins.)*

**Framing device:** Tonto has been narrating the history of the Metabarons to Lothar, but Lothar wants to know more about the origins of the clan's founding father — Dayal of Castaka — and where the crest of Castaka, which is transferred from father to son, originally comes from. Tonto begins the story. The story is set at a time when Lothar still inhabits his normal body and is not yet aware of his real identity, placing it before the events of Aghora the Father-Mother.

**Flashback — The Castaka clan on Ahour:** The noble Castaka clan lives happily on Ahour, a small fertile planet, until the nascent Techno-techno guild builds a planet for their Techno-pope in the same orbit around the double sun Kar-kar. Fearing that Ahour might fall to enemies and become a base for attacks, the Technoguild decides to disintegrate it, exterminating the entire population — with the exception of Baron Dayal of Castaka, his wife Duchess Antigrea, and their twin daughters Myrtha and Narda, who manage to escape.

**Arrival on Marmola and Antigrea's death:** The Castaka family reaches the distant marble planet of Marmola in the Philidor system. According to the Bushitaka code, Antigrea must be buried, but Marmola's marble surface makes this impossible. When Dayal is about to crash his mini-vessel to break the marble, they are surrounded by three thieving geologists — Kouhry, Lahry, and Mohry, known as the "Three Aces." They are hunting a precious mineral beneath the marble surface, guarded by Gangez, the Millennial Angel Vulture. The thieves plan to use Antigrea's corpse to bait the megabird, capture him, and force him to reveal the treasure. When Gangez takes the bait the Three Aces begin torturing him, leaving him near death. Dayal turns the situation to his advantage, shoots the thieves, and feeds them to Gangez.

**The Crest:** The megabird — who now carries Antigrea within him and is near death — senses the noble vibrations of Dayal's spirit. Having faith in the baron's goodness, Gangez promises to show them Marmola's only valley of fertile earth where he can be buried. Dayal buries Gangez following the Bushitaka code. Impressed by the Castaka's generosity, Gangez offers his soul to Dayal so that he can live within him, serving as master and guardian of the planet and its treasure. Dayal agrees, and Gangez transfers his crest into Dayal's chest. The Castakas then build their new home over his grave, and the soul of Gangez guides them to the heart of the planet where they discover the epyphite.

## 10. Weapons of the Metabaron
**One-off graphic novel | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Travis Charest and Zoran Janjetov | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 2008**
*(Travis Charest completed 29 pages before leaving the industry around 1999–2000; those pages were released in 2002 as "The Last Metabaron." The project was then left on hold until Zoran Janjetov completed it. The story takes place after Aghora the Father-Mother and partly overlaps with Nameless, the Last of the Metabarons, but before Before the Incal.)*

**The patricide:** The Metabaron and his father-mother Aghora return from a victorious battle against the Karbit armada to Okhar. Upon arrival, Nameless fights a leodactylea to prove his strength. After the beast is defeated, the initiatory tradition commences: the son must kill his father in combat. Nameless succeeds. Aghora transfers the crest of the Castaka to him before dying. The Metabaron swears never to have children, launches Aghora's coffin into space, and triggers Aghora's Oko-bombs.

**The secret of the Castakas:** Now that Aghora is dead, Tonto tells the Metabaron the last secret of the Castakas: the location of Omphale, at the center of Enneade. Inside it, eight sages who are 300,000 years old rest in the sleeping room, fed by the energy of Ennead. The Ennead is surrounded by the main dreamer Bihargam, creator of all visions that emerge at night for living beings.

**Praxis — the Sword with a Soul:** Nameless meets the sages, who guide him into Ennead's dream. Within the dream he traces the Virgin Temple and acquires Praxis, a sword with a soul. He faces the wicked door, where a Hulzgemeaux attacks him. Praxis helps by filling the warrior's heart with the sword's soul, allowing him to close the door and re-enter the normal realm. The sages let him keep Praxis and inform him there are still many Hulzgemeaux in Enneade, three opponents he must overcome, and two more weapons to acquire.

**The omnigrail:** The next weapon Nameless must find is the transpinal eye — a mini-computer with its own will that escaped — but first he must activate the omnigrail, held by a tribe of cannibal lizard-men on Bottra. The Metabaron arrives, frees aboriginal child prisoners, drives Praxis into the ground, and uses his Superkolt to destroy the lizard-men's temple and kill them all. He is then attacked by a giant spider, which he defeats using Praxis. He acquires the omnigrail.

**The transpinal eye in Trafalhar:** Nameless sets course for the concentric rings of Trafalhar to find the transpinal eye. In the first ring, the metacraft is attacked by a formless-matter monster — defeated using Praxis's vibration. In the second ring, egg-asteroids release fire-breathing dragons; the Metabaron uses an invulnerable shield to defend himself, then launches a super Oko-missile at the brain-asteroid of all dragons, killing them all. In the third ring, each asteroid contains a colony of leeches.

## 11. Dayal, the First Ancestor
**Issue #1 of *Castaka* | Written by Alejandro Jodorowsky | Illustrated by Das Pastoras | Les Humanoïdes Associés, 2007**
*(The English collected edition, Metabaron Genesis: Castaka, adds extra panels at the end. The story of the Castaka is narrated by Berard of Castaka to Othon Von Salza just before Marmola is attacked, as depicted in Othon the Great-Great-Grandfather.)*

**Framing device:** Before enemy forces arrive on Marmola to steal the epyphane, Berard gathers his family. He wants Othon to be his heir and fights him to test his ability to defend himself. Othon pins Berard down, but Berard tricks Othon and defeats him. By Bushitaka law Berard should kill him; however, recognizing that Othon fought fairly while he did not, Berard spares him.

**Flashback — The Amakura war and the origin of Dayal:** The Amakura clan wages war against the Castaka on Ahour. The Castaka prince Divadal — aware he is losing — takes as prisoner Oriela, the wife of the Amakura king Omezo. Divadal impregnates Oriela, then asks her to kill him now that he has managed to end the Castaka bloodline with this act. Following Bushitaka law, King Omezo should demand Oriela's immediate suicide after liberation, but since she is the only woman pregnant with a fertile Castaka male, they wait for her to give birth. After several months she does, naming the boy Dayal and leaving the scar of her teeth on him as a memento. When the child is eight years old, Oriela ritually commits suicide.

**Dayal's upbringing:** Dayal grows within the Castaka palace, cared for by dozens of servants and educated by General Pakko. Eventually he begins ritually inseminating three Castaka women every night for twelve years, so that the clan can become fertile again. The forced, loveless sex makes him a cruel lover. At age 26 a ceremony takes place to grant him the title of Baron, attended by all the women he has impregnated and their children.

**Dayal's crisis of identity:** King Omezo nominates Dayal as baron, but in a fit of disgust tells him to leave immediately. When General Pakko reveals to Dayal the truth about his father, Dayal goes mad and isolates himself in the palace. Pakko then informs him that he has a daughter — Antigrea, a duchess of the same age who has been trained in combat by Pakko himself. After a year of isolation Pakko brings Antigrea to Dayal. Initially they reject each other, but eventually fall in love. Antigrea bears him twin daughters, Myrtha and Narda, but is left barren afterwards. Pakko trains the twins in combat in the same manner as Antigrea.

**The Techno-techno threat and the destruction of Ahour:** The Techno-techno sect begins constructing an artificial planet — the future secret home of the Techno-pope — in the same orbit as Ahour. Hoping to replace King Omezo with someone more welcoming to the Technos, the cult secretly infects Omezo with paleo-tuberculosis. Sensing his death approaching, Omezo orders Dayal's assassination so the throne can pass to an old guard soldier. Pakko warns Dayal's family and tells them to hide. Dayal takes his family to his hunter's den with supplies. Pakko stays behind to delay the assassins but is ultimately murdered. The Techno-technos then decide to blow up the entire planet. Miraculously, Dayal's family survives because the cave where they were hidden was embedded in a piece of rock that remains intact, and they are now drifting in space.
