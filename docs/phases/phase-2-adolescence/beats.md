# Phase 2 Major Beats

The available narrative beats in the adolescence arc. Like [Phase 1 beats](../phase-1-vertical-slice/beats.md), none is forced. The Narrator selects which land based on player evolution + scene context. Phase 2 spans Sans-Nom ages 12–17 across multiple locations and sessions.

## Beat A — First full Bushitaka lesson

**Trigger.** Aghora (or another figure the Narrator places in the role) begins formal training in earnest. Typically after Sans-Nom has demonstrated some form of readiness — or after he has demonstrated that readiness is not coming on its own and must be forced.

**Stakes.** The form is no longer abstract. Sans-Nom learns what it prepares him for. The Narrator must not over-explain; the *weight* of the knowledge should arrive as a feeling, not an announcement. "This is how you kill someone" does not need to be said.

**Variation.** Aghora may teach with precision and remove. A generated figure (a sparring partner, a visiting warrior) may teach with cruelty or with unexpected gentleness. The form of the lesson shapes Sans-Nom's relationship to the Bushitaka for the remainder of the playthrough.

## Beat B — First death

**Trigger.** The Phase 2 death state activates. Sans-Nom can die in this phase — this is the first phase where death is mechanically possible. The trigger is Narrator-decided, based on scene logic and accumulated choices.

**Stakes.** The most load-bearing single event of Phase 2. The [death state](../../mechanics/player-character/death-state.md) design must land exactly right: Tonto's eulogy, the archive naming, the clean close. Proof 4 of Phase 2 exit criteria is measured here.

**Variation.** Not every Phase 2 playthrough ends in death. Most do not. But the Narrator must be willing to use the death state when it is earned. A Sans-Nom who refuses every form of protection and walks into violence is not to be protected by authorial mercy.

## Beat C — First location-arc transition

**Trigger.** Sans-Nom leaves the compound. This is the first genuine session boundary — the end of one arc, the opening of another.

**Stakes.** Multi-session continuity is exercised for the first time. Characters who were left behind at the compound must remember what happened there when they next appear. The session boundary must land as a narrative beat, not a technical seam — "Sans-Nom does not see the compound again for two years" should feel like two years.

**Variation.** Where Sans-Nom goes and why depends entirely on the playthrough's accumulated pressure. He might be sent somewhere, flee somewhere, or be taken somewhere. All three are in-character outcomes depending on who he has become.

## Beat D — Encounter with the older order

**Trigger.** Sans-Nom meets a figure connected to the Imperial machinery, the Halerce remnants, or some other echo of the civilization the dynasty exists inside. The Narrator generates this figure.

**Stakes.** The first time Sans-Nom understands the dynasty as a *small* thing inside a *large* world. The IP's scale is operatic; Phase 1 was claustrophobically compound-bound. This beat opens the frame.

**Variation.** The figure might be hostile (a bounty hunter; an imperial agent who knows the Metabarons' name), indifferent (a merchant who has stories), or reverent (someone who has heard of Aghora and treats Sans-Nom accordingly). Each inflection shapes how Sans-Nom understands his inheritance.

## Beat E — The eastern hall fully revealed

**Trigger.** Sans-Nom learns who is in the stasis pod. This may happen through Tonto, through Aghora finally breaking, through the stasis pod itself if he found it in Phase 1, or through an encounter with someone who knew Steelhead.

**Stakes.** Sans-Nom's grandfather is sealed in madness because his grandmother died and his parent could not kill him. This is the dynasty's wound made visible. The Narrator must let the revelation arrive at the speed it deserves — not rushed to plot, not withheld past when it has been earned.

**Variation.** What Sans-Nom *does* with this knowledge is the variation. He might rage. He might become quieter. He might ask Tonto to tell him everything. He might go back to the compound and stand outside the eastern hall. All are in-character. The Narrator follows the boy.

## Beat F — Aghora's grief surfaces (without naming its source)

**Trigger.** A moment — typically quiet, unexpected — where Aghora's grief about Steelhead shows through the precision and control. She does not name Steelhead. She does not explain the mercy. But something she does or fails to do reveals the wound's shape.

**Stakes.** The load-bearing Phase 1 secret (Aghora's wound) lands in Phase 2 not as an explanation but as a feeling. The player understands something they were not told. This is the game's central authoring discipline: [negative space](../../principles/player-fantasy.md).

**Variation.** The form of the surface depends on the playthrough's tone. It might be Aghora sitting with Sans-Nom after a difficult training session and saying something she immediately walks back. It might be a single moment of stillness when Sans-Nom mentions the eastern hall. It might be Tonto's long story about Aghora as a child — which contains more than Tonto intended.

## Cross-beat principles (Phase 2)

- Beats A–F are all available across the phase; none is required; none has a fixed order.
- Some beats (B, C) are architectural firsts — the Narrator must be confident in the implementation before letting them land.
- Beat B (death) should not arrive before the player has had time to care about this Sans-Nom. The Narrator does not kill early.
- The beats are not milestones. Phase 2 is not a progression system. The Narrator uses beats as *potential*; the player's choices and the playthrough's accumulated tone determine which potential is realized.
- Beat E (eastern hall revealed) is the natural precursor to Phase 3's Steelhead encounter. A playthrough that reaches Phase 3 without Beat E landing will need to compress. The Narrator may decide to run Beat E early in Phase 3 if Phase 2 never delivered it.
