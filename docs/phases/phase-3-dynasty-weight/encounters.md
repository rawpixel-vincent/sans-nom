# Phase 3 Pivotal Encounters

The three climactic moments of the game. Each is available in Phase 3; none is guaranteed to land in every playthrough. The Narrator decides when — and whether — each encounter occurs, based on the playthrough's accumulated weight.

These are the game's load-bearing dramatic events. The IP requires they be handled with full authorial seriousness.

## Encounter 1 — The chest-crest inheritance

### What it is

Sans-Nom receives the dynasty's crest. Through it, ancestor peers' representations of him become directly readable to him — he can in some sense converse with the dead. See [dead-and-absent.md](../../mechanics/cast/dead-and-absent.md) and [Pillar 2](../../principles/pillars/02-characters-witness-bound.md).

### Trigger

The Narrator decides when Sans-Nom is ready to receive it. There is no automatic trigger. Likely conditions: Sans-Nom has reached some level of Bushitaka understanding; the dynasty's weight is bearing down; a moment of stillness creates the right frame. The crest may be given by Aghora, found in the compound, or arrive through a Tonto story. The *form* of the giving is a Narrator decision; the weight of the moment is non-negotiable.

### Available forms

- **Aghora presents it.** The most direct form. She explains nothing; she gives him the crest. The silence around the giving is the explanation.
- **Found in the eastern hall.** After learning about Steelhead, Sans-Nom finds the crest among the preserved objects. No ceremony. The receiving is quiet.
- **Tonto's story leads to it.** Tonto narrates a scene involving the crest; the narrative lands Sans-Nom in possession of it.

### How it feels in play

The first time Sans-Nom uses the crest, an ancestor speaks. Not through narrated memory — directly, in their voice, using what they actually knew of *this* Sans-Nom. The witness-bound logic holds: Honorata has a different view of Sans-Nom than Othon; Vicenta's voice carries a gentleness the others do not.

The Narrator must resist writing the ancestors as lesson-dispensers. They are themselves: wounded, specific, bounded by what they witnessed. Honorata does not tell Sans-Nom how to be a Metabaron; she tells him what she noticed about him, which is a different and more frightening thing.

### What the Narrator must avoid

- Treating the crest as a power-up. It is not a tool; it is an inheritance.
- Writing the ancestors as a chorus of approval or judgment. They simply *are* — specific, bounded, real.
- Overusing it. The first crest conversation is the most important. Give it room.

### Stakes

The chest-crest is the architectural culmination of [Pillar 2](../../principles/pillars/02-characters-witness-bound.md). Every directional representation built across the playthrough is now accessible to Sans-Nom. If the representations are rich, the crest conversations are rich. If they are thin, the conversations will be thin. This is the design working correctly — the player's playthrough is the content.

## Encounter 2 — The Aghora-mercy revelation

### What it is

Sans-Nom learns that his parent could not kill Steelhead — that the Bushitaka was broken, the dynasty's law violated, and that he himself is the consequence of that mercy. This is Aghora's defining wound made legible.

### Trigger

Available throughout Phase 3, but should not arrive before Sans-Nom has enough context for its weight to land. Prerequisites: Beat E (eastern hall revealed) should have landed. Some understanding of the Bushitaka should exist. Aghora's grief should have surfaced at least once (Beat F, Phase 2).

The revelation may come from Aghora directly (a rare moment of unburdening), from Tonto (whose representation of this event is secondhand but rich), from the crest (Steelhead's version of events is haunting), or from a found record.

### How it lands

The Narrator must resist the temptation to write this as a confession scene. Aghora does not confess. She does not apologize. If she tells Sans-Nom, she tells him the way she tells him everything: with precision, without softness, and with the full weight of what she has spent his entire childhood not saying.

The moment immediately raises the question it does not answer: *will Sans-Nom do the same?* The Narrator does not ask this question aloud. The player feels it arrive.

### Available forms

- **Aghora tells him.** Direct, spare, in the compound. A single conversation. Probably brief.
- **The crest tells him.** Steelhead's representation of Aghora — filtered through his own wound and his broken perception — describes the mercy from the other side. This version is less clear and more devastating.
- **Tonto tells him.** Tonto's version is secondhand: "This is what Aghora told me, years later, when she was tired." It carries Tonto's narrative voice over an event he did not witness.
- **A record.** Some account Aghora left — a message, a log — that Sans-Nom finds. She wrote it when Sans-Nom was very young, expecting she might die before she told him herself.

### What the Narrator must avoid

- Framing the mercy as a mistake to be corrected. Whether it was mercy or cowardice is the IP's unresolved question; the game does not resolve it.
- Letting Sans-Nom react too cleanly. This revelation should sit in him for scenes afterward, not be processed in a single response.
- Delivering it too early. The revelation lands heavier the more the player has come to understand the dynasty's weight.

### Stakes

This is the hinge between Phase 2's slow accumulation and Phase 3's Bushitaka decision. After this beat, the question is live: *will Sans-Nom kill Aghora?* Everything the player has built becomes the answer to that question.

## Encounter 3 — The Steelhead encounter

### What it is

Sans-Nom encounters his grandfather: the man Aghora could not kill, sealed in the eastern hall, mad and preserved. The encounter may be live (if Sans-Nom has broken the stasis), through the crest, or never (a valid outcome).

### Trigger

Depends entirely on the playthrough. If Sans-Nom found the eastern hall in Phase 1 or Phase 2 and pursued it, the encounter may come naturally. If he avoided the hall his entire life, the encounter may not happen at all — and Steelhead remains a presence without a face.

The Narrator decides whether to offer the encounter. It should not be forced on a player whose Sans-Nom chose not to know.

### Available forms

- **Stasis broken.** If Sans-Nom or some event has broken the stasis, Steelhead is awake in some form. What form depends on how deeply the Melmoth descent has taken hold by the time of waking. The encounter is disorienting, not triumphant.
- **Through the crest.** Steelhead's representation of Sans-Nom — assembled from whatever Steelhead witnessed before the sealing — is partial and strange. He may not recognize his grandson by name. He knows him by gesture, by posture, by the shape of his training.
- **Stasis observed, not broken.** The most restrained form: Sans-Nom finds the pod, sees what is inside, and makes no move to open it. The encounter is visual only. What he does with that seeing is the event.
- **Never.** A valid playthrough can end without Sans-Nom meeting Steelhead at all. The eastern hall stays locked. Steelhead remains in the architecture but not in the story. The game does not require this encounter.

### How it feels

Steelhead's voice changes by phase (see [steelhead.md](../../mechanics/cast/steelhead.md)): early is martial and decisive; late is fragmentary and pleading. In Phase 3, the voice is primarily late-Steelhead — broken by the Melmoth, drifting between clarity and its absence. The actor agent handles this through the phase-flag system; the Narrator frames the encounter to match the flag.

The player should feel pulled toward and afraid simultaneously. This is a grandfather who loves and a man who is broken. The two are not separable.

### What the Narrator must avoid

- Writing Steelhead as a redemption arc. He is broken. The game does not promise repair.
- Giving the encounter a clear emotional resolution. Players who want clean feelings will not find them here.
- Forcing the encounter. If the player's Sans-Nom genuinely did not pursue this knowledge, the encounter is not owed.

### Stakes

If this encounter happens, it is likely the last time Sans-Nom sees what the Bushitaka does to the people who fail to perform it. He is looking at the cost of his parent's mercy — embodied, preserved, still breathing. The Bushitaka decision, if it has not yet landed on him, will feel closer after this.

## Cross-encounter principles

- All three encounters are available throughout Phase 3; none has a fixed order.
- Encounter 1 (chest-crest) typically enables richer versions of Encounters 2 and 3 — the ancestors' voices add texture to both revelations. But it is not a prerequisite.
- Encounter 2 (Aghora-mercy) is the narrative hinge. The Bushitaka decision does not need to land in the same session, but it needs to land *after* the player understands what the mercy cost.
- Encounter 3 (Steelhead) may never happen. That is a valid shape. Not every Sans-Nom meets his grandfather.
- The Bushitaka decision itself — whether Sans-Nom performs the kill, refuses, or dies before choosing — is not a fourth "encounter." It is the accumulated weight of everything above. The Narrator does not design it; it arrives from the playthrough.
