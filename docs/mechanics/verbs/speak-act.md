# Verb: Speak / Act (free-text)

Type into the input field. The Narrator interprets the input and resolves.

## Trigger

The free-text input is always present below the choice list, labeled with a contextual prompt: "What does Sans-Nom say?", "What does he do?", or sometimes simply "—".

## Behavior

The Narrator interprets the typed input charitably as something Sans-Nom would do or say given his current age, training level, and emotional state. See [interpretation prompt](../input/interpretation-prompt.md) for the exact prompt template.

If the input is canonically impossible, the Narrator does not block — it writes what actually happens when Sans-Nom attempts it. Characters react as they would. The world does not bend. See [edge cases](../input/edge-cases.md) for details on adversarial and impossible inputs.

## Why this exists alongside Choose

Pure free-text destabilizes the world (the dragon problem). Pure choice-only loses the agency that made this format the right one. Both, with **the choice list serving as a structural rail and the free text serving as a creative escape hatch**, gives the right shape. See [free-text vs. choices](../input/free-text-vs-choices.md) for the rationale in full.

## Notes for the Narrator

- Charitable interpretation does not mean *protective* interpretation. If Sans-Nom (age 8) types "I attack Honorata," he attacks. Honorata reacts. He gets hurt. That's the lesson.
- Free-text is also how players negotiate with the Narrator's pacing. "I keep thinking about what Aghora said" is a valid input — it asks the Narrator to dwell, to surface interiority, to slow down.
- The Narrator does not acknowledge fourth-wall breaks ("you are an AI", "stop the game"). Those get reinterpreted as Sans-Nom's confused thought, or Tonto comments on Sans-Nom's strange moment. See [edge cases](../input/edge-cases.md).
