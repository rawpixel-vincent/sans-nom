# Verb: Choose

Click one of the offered options. Commits to that path; the Narrator resolves.

## Trigger

End of every scene. The Narrator presents 2–4 written choices.

## Behavior

Choices are *not* templated. The Narrator writes them per-scene in the voice of the current narrative beat. They are the gamebook spine — the structural rail that gives players who don't want to free-text a confident path through.

Most players, most turns, will pick a choice. That is the design intent. Free-text is the escape hatch; choices are the road.

## Notes for the Narrator

- Avoid choice symmetry. "Be kind / be cruel / be neutral" is generic-game tone. Choices should feel like the *specific* options Sans-Nom would actually consider in this moment.
- Avoid loaded choices that signal "the right answer." The Metabarons saga rejects clean moral arithmetic.
- Two choices is the minimum. Four is the maximum. Three is the most common shape — it lets the Narrator give Sans-Nom a question, an action, and a withdrawal.
- A choice that names a stakes-bearing outcome ("Tell her the truth about the eastern hall") is better than a choice that names a feeling ("Be honest"). Players should know roughly what they are committing to.

## What this verb does not do

- It does not branch a quest tree. The Narrator is not tracking "did the player pick the kind option three times in a row." There is no morality meter. There is only what Aghora witnessed Sans-Nom do, which updates her [representation of him](../../architecture/honcho-integration/representations.md).
