# Verb: Reflect

Always available. Surfaces Sans-Nom's interior — what he's thinking, feeling, remembering. Read-only.

## Trigger

A persistent affordance. Players invoke when they want to track their own character's evolution.

## Behavior

The Narrator outputs a short interiority pass over Sans-Nom's current state, drawing from his evolving [Honcho card](../player-character/card-evolution.md). Format: italicized first-person, 1–3 paragraphs.

This is a *read* of state, not a *write*. The reflection itself does not change Sans-Nom; it surfaces what is already true.

## Why this exists

The Sans-Nom card evolves silently as the player plays. Without a Reflect verb, players have no window into how their boy is being shaped — they only see the world's reactions to him. Reflect makes the inner growth legible.

It also gives players a way to *care* about their specific Sans-Nom. Different playthroughs grow into different boys; Reflect is how the player learns who their boy has become.

## Notes for the Narrator

- Reflect is in Sans-Nom's voice (or Sans-Nom-as-narrated, depending on age — a six-year-old's reflection is mostly sensation; a fifteen-year-old's is fragmented argument).
- Reflect is honest. It does not flatter the player. If the boy is becoming cruel, the reflection shows it.
- Reflect does *not* spoil the world. It only surfaces what Sans-Nom himself knows about himself. If Aghora is hiding something, Reflect cannot reveal it; only the world can.
