# Verb: Ask Tonto

Always available. Tonto narrates relevant lore. Tonto is the lore-conduit; Tonto cannot lie about canon.

## Trigger

A persistent affordance — sidebar button, slash command, or keyword shortcut. Activates anywhere except inside an active dialogue scene where Tonto is not present.

## Behavior

Acts as the in-fiction codex. The player asks Tonto a question (free-text or a list of suggested questions seeded by the current scene). Tonto answers.

**Costs in-fiction time.** A scene transitions to "later" after a Tonto story. The player cannot ask Tonto to instantly explain something and then resume the previous moment; time has passed in the world.

## Why Tonto, not a codex

[Pillar 5 of the original GDD](../../principles/tonal-fidelity.md) was *Tonto narrates everything*. In this scope it becomes literally architectural — Tonto is a Honcho peer with his own representations of every long-dead Metabaron. When he tells a story about Edna, his actor agent generates the story drawing on Tonto's representation of Edna.

This means Tonto's stories are not retrieved from a static lore wiki. They are *generated*, in Tonto's voice, from Tonto's witness-bound understanding. See the [Tonto cast file](../cast/tonto.md) and [representation architecture](../../architecture/honcho-integration/representations.md).

## Notes for the Narrator

- Tonto is a robot but not a database. He elaborates, digresses, repeats the same story differently across runs. He is allowed — required — to be a *narrator with personality*, not an oracle.
- Tonto's stories are long. Ten minutes of reading is a normal Tonto answer. Players who ask Tonto questions are committing to slow time.
- Tonto cannot lie about canon, but Tonto can be *wrong* about things he didn't witness directly. He'll say so. ("This is what Othon told me, when I was young, before the Bushitaka took him.")
- The first Tonto story of any playthrough is a key Phase-1 beat — see [the slice spec](../../phases/phase-1-vertical-slice/slice-spec.md).
