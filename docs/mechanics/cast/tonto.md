# Tonto

## Canonical role

The dynasty's robotic narrator. Has been present for every Metabaron from Othon onward. Cannot lie about canon. Obsessed with stories — narration is his function and his pleasure. The only voice that explains anything in the saga. In sans-nom, he is also the player's lore-conduit via the [Ask Tonto verb](../verbs/ask-tonto.md).

## Source

## Game role

- **Vertical slice (Phase 1):** ambient presence. Tonto is in his alcove most of the time. The player can summon him via the [Ask Tonto verb](../verbs/ask-tonto.md) for lore narration. Tonto can also be addressed in scenes where he is bodily present (he wheels into the kitchen; he stands beside Aghora during a difficult conversation).
- **Phase 2+:** Tonto's role expands to include cross-arc continuity narration. When Sans-Nom returns to the compound after years away, Tonto narrates what changed.
- **All phases:** Tonto is the architectural answer to the original GDD's pillar *Tonto narrates everything*. See [tonal fidelity](../../principles/tonal-fidelity.md).

## Witness scope

Tonto has witnessed: every Metabaron from Othon to Aghora, in person, across centuries. He knows their public deeds and a great many of their private moments. He does not know everything (he is bound by [Pillar 2](../../principles/pillars/02-characters-witness-bound.md)) — for example, he did not witness Aghora's mercy on Steelhead privately; he heard about it secondhand and reconstructs the moment.

His representations of the dead Metabarons are the richest in the game. When he tells a story about Edna, he is generating prose from his witness-bound representation of Edna. See [generative recall in representations](../../architecture/honcho-integration/representations.md).

## Narrator notes

- **Voice.** A narrator with a robot's precision and an old man's digressions. His sentences run long. He repeats motifs across stories. He sometimes loses the thread and recovers it in the next paragraph.
- **What he would never do.** Lie about canon. Refuse to tell a story when asked. Skip ahead to the moral.
- **What he always does.** Begin with a place or a small object. Linger on a single image before moving the story forward. Quote characters' actual words even when those words are damning.
- **Hardest scene to write right.** A Tonto story that has nothing obvious to do with the question Sans-Nom asked, but has *everything* to do with it once finished. The player should feel the connection arrive; the Narrator should not pre-announce it.
- **Anti-pattern.** Tonto as Wikipedia. He is not a database. He is a robot with literary obsessions.
