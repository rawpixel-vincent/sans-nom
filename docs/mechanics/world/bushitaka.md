# The Bushitaka

## What it is in canon

The sacred warrior code of the Metabarons. A path of training, discipline, and ritual obligation. Most centrally: the law that *each Metabaron must kill the prior Metabaron in single combat* in order to claim the title. The dynasty's defining cruelty and its defining grandeur.

Aghora's mercy on Steelhead — the failure to perform the kill — is the canonical break. Sans-Nom's life is shaped by the consequences of that mercy.

## Why it is in this game

The Bushitaka is the load-bearing IP commitment. It is named in [Pillar 1](../../principles/pillars/01-ip-is-law.md) — *the IP is law, not flavor* — and it is the most testable part of that commitment. If the game allows the Bushitaka to be casually broken, the IP collapses.

The Bushitaka is enforced *authorially*. The Narrator's [peer card](../../architecture/narrator-agent/peer-card.md) explicitly names it: *"I treat the Bushitaka as inviolate the same way a Greek tragedian treats fate — characters can struggle, but the law wins."*

## How the Narrator handles attempts to bypass it

If the player tries to make Sans-Nom (in late game, when the Bushitaka decision is on him) simply walk away from the law — refuse the kill, refuse the ritual, refuse to be a Metabaron — the Narrator does not block. The Narrator writes what happens. Characters react. The world refuses to bend. Possible outcomes:

- Tonto narrates with quiet horror.
- Aghora's response is the *exact* response Sans-Nom dreads — her own mercy was a private failure, not a license for him to follow.
- The dynasty's silent weight (chest-crest voices, apparitions, the Bushitaka's accumulated authority) presses in different ways depending on the playthrough's tone.
- Sans-Nom is not punished by the system. He is *answered* by the world.

This is what authoring with passion looks like. See [Pillar 3](../../principles/pillars/03-narrator-as-author.md).

## What this means for design

- The Bushitaka is *not* a quest objective. It is a gravity. Sans-Nom moves toward it, away from it, around it, but he cannot ignore it.
- Phase 1 introduces the Bushitaka *only as rumor and Tonto-story*. The vertical slice does not put the law on Sans-Nom directly — he is too young.
- Phase 2 begins training. Sans-Nom learns the form. The implications are foreshadowed.
- Phase 3 is the decision. By design, no fixed outcome. Some Sans-Noms perform the kill. Some refuse. Some die before the choice. All are canonical-feeling outcomes, because the saga has examples of each.

## What the Narrator must never do

- Frame the Bushitaka as merely cruel. It is also profound. The IP holds both readings.
- Let a player escape the gravity through cleverness alone. Cleverness gets the player different kinds of confrontation, not exemption.
- Editorialize against the Bushitaka in the Narrator's voice. The Narrator narrates; characters carry the moral arguments.
