# Why This Is the Right Game for This Team

The user's existing assets:

- Eight character lore cards, written with the depth of literary criticism — at `external/references/council of castaka/metabarons/`.
- A complete directional relationship matrix encoding refracted knowledge — at `external/references/council of castaka/metabarons/relations/`.
- A working agent-orchestration system (Council of Castaka) that demonstrates per-character pressure-prompt design and multi-agent deliberation.
- 446 Metabarons comic page scans at `external/comics/meta-barons/`.
- Deep personal familiarity with the IP, including the willingness to treat it as serious creative material.

The game this implies is not a souls-like. It's the game whose architecture is *exactly* what the user has already built, scaled up and turned into a player-facing experience. The Council of Castaka is, in an architectural sense, a Phase-0 prototype of this game's actor-orchestration layer. The character cards are the actor card seeds. The relationship matrix is the directional-representation seed.

A 4-person team building this is leveraging 80% of work the user has already done. The risk is technical execution and tonal discipline, not material creation.

## What this rules out

- A 3D souls-like adaptation. That's the right design at AAA scale; it is not the right design at indie scale with these assets.
- A from-scratch IP. The leverage from existing assets is too high to discard.
- A pivot to a different IP. The matched-shape between Honcho's directional representations and the Metabarons' refracted-knowledge canon is the project's core insight; a generic IP loses it.

## What this enables

- 8-month playable beta, 14-month v1.0 with 4 people. Reasonable.
- Per-character authoring depth that AAA games rarely achieve, because the author is one person inheriting eight already-deep cards.
- Reusability: the engine generalizes. Future titles in other comic IPs reuse 80% of the engineering. See the [risk register](../project/risks/register.md) on player-base sizing.
