# Pillar 4 — Replay is the medium

A game without a fixed ending state is a game that requires replay to matter. The system is designed to make second and third runs structurally different — different first encounters, different ancestors visited, different versions of the player's Sans-Nom growing into different shapes.

## What this means in practice

- There is no win condition. There is only what Sans-Nom becomes before he dies.
- Each playthrough is its own [Honcho workspace](../../mechanics/player-character/multiple-sans-noms.md). Different Sans-Noms grow up differently. Replay-as-fresh-character is the canonical-feeling mechanic — the Metabarons saga is *about* the failure of cycles to repeat exactly.
- Variation is not bolted-on (random encounters, branching quests). It is structural — emerging from per-character actor agents responding to *this* Sans-Nom's evolving representation.

## What this rules out

- New Game+ that carries over abilities or items. Wrong for the IP.
- A "true ending" that completionists chase. Wrong for the design.
- Procedurally-shuffled content that targets variety as a metric. Variety is a side effect of authored response to player input, not a target.

## What depends on this

- The [multiple-Sans-Noms model](../../mechanics/player-character/multiple-sans-noms.md) — workspace per playthrough, not save slots in a single workspace.
- The [card evolution](../../mechanics/player-character/card-evolution.md) — the player's character literally grows up in the Honcho card; that growth is the save state.
- Death-state design (Phase 2+) — what happens when Sans-Nom dies determines whether replay feels like a new beginning or a defeat. Designed for the former.
